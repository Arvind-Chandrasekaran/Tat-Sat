# DQS

### **1. The Discourse Quality Score (DQS)**

The Discourse Quality Score (DQS) is a value between 0 and 1, assigned to each piece of content, C. It is a weighted sum of four component scores:

**DQS(C) = w_a * ASQ(C) + w_f * FS(C) + w_c * CPS(C) + w_e * ETS(C)**

where:

- ASQ(C) is the Argument Structure Quality score.
- FS(C) is the Fallacy Score.
- CPS(C) is the Cooperative Principle Score.
- ETS(C) is the Emotional Tone Score.
- w_a, w_f, w_c, and w_e are the weights assigned to each component, with the constraint that w_a + w_f + w_c + w_e = 1. These weights can be adjusted based on empirical data to optimize the algorithm's performance.

**1.1 Argument Structure Quality (ASQ)**

The ASQ is based on the Toulmin model of argumentation and is calculated as follows:

**ASQ(C) = (c + g*w_g + w*w_w + b*w_b + q*w_q + r*w_r) / (1 + w_g + w_w + w_b + w_q + w_r)**

where each variable represents the presence (1) or absence (0) of a component, identified through Natural Language Processing (NLP):

- **c**: Claim (the baseline for any argument)
- **g**: Grounds (evidence)
- **w**: Warrant (logical link)
- **b**: Backing (support for the warrant)
- **q**: Qualifier (nuanced language)
- **r**: Rebuttal (addressing counter-arguments)

The weights (w_g, w_w, w_b, w_q, w_r) represent the relative importance of each component. For example, a claim with grounds is more valuable than a claim alone.

**1.2 Fallacy Score (FS)**

The FS is a measure of the presence of logical fallacies, where a higher score indicates fewer fallacies:

**FS(C) = 1 - ( Σ (f_i * w_i) / n )**

where:

- **n** is the number of fallacies being scanned for.
- **f_i** is a binary value indicating the presence (1) or absence (0) of fallacy *i*.
- **w_i** is the weight assigned to fallacy *i*, representing its severity. For example, an *ad hominem* attack might be weighted more heavily than a weaker fallacy.

**1.3 Cooperative Principle Score (CPS)**

The CPS is based on Grice's Maxims and is an average of its four sub-scores:

**CPS(C) = (MQl(C) + MQn(C) + MR(C) + MM(C)) / 4**

where:

- **MQl(C)**: Maxim of Quality - a score based on cross-referencing with fact-checking databases and identifying citations.
- **MQn(C)**: Maxim of Quantity - a score measuring the information density and novelty of the content.
- **MR(C)**: Maxim of Relevance - calculated using semantic similarity (e.g., cosine similarity) between the content and the parent post or topic.
- **MM(C)**: Maxim of Manner - a score based on readability metrics (e.g., Flesch-Kincaid) and the absence of ambiguity.

**1.4 Emotional Tone Score (ETS)**

The ETS differentiates between constructive and inflammatory emotional language:

**ETS(C) = 1 - (I(C) * (1 - ASQ(C)))**

where:

- **I(C)** is the intensity of the emotional language in the content, as determined by sentiment analysis tools (scaled from 0 to 1).
- **ASQ(C)** is the previously calculated Argument Structure Quality.

This formula ensures that highly emotional content without a strong logical structure is penalized, while emotionally resonant content that is part of a well-formed argument is not.

### **2. The Contributor Score (CS)**

The Contributor Score (CS) is a value between 0 and 1 assigned to each user, U, reflecting their history of contributions to the platform:

**CS(U) = (1 - α) * avg(DQS(U_posts)) + α * ( Σ (I_ij * CS(U_j)) / Σ I_ij )**

where:

- **avg(DQS(U_posts))** is the user's average DQS across all their posts.
- **α** is a weighting factor that balances a user's own contributions with how the community interacts with them.
- **I_ij** is the interaction value of user *j* with a post from user *i*. This could be a binary value for a "quality upvote" or a more nuanced score.
- **CS(U_j)** is the Contributor Score of the interacting user.
- The second term is a weighted average of the scores of users who have positively interacted with the user's content, making endorsements from high-quality contributors more valuable.

The CS is recalculated periodically to reflect a user's ongoing contributions.

### **3. The Content Visibility Score (CVS)**

The final Content Visibility Score (CVS) determines how a piece of content is ranked and distributed across the platform:

**CVS(C) = β * DQS(C) + (1 - β) * CS(U_c) + γ * E(C) + δ * T**

where:

- **DQS(C)** is the Discourse Quality Score of the content.
- **CS(U_c)** is the Contributor Score of the user who posted the content.
- **E(C)** is a weighted engagement score. This is where traditional metrics like upvotes and shares are considered, but with a significantly lower weight. Quality interactions (e.g., high-DQS replies) are given a higher weight within E(C).
- **T** is a time decay factor, to ensure that newer content is also surfaced.
- **β, (1-β), γ,** and **δ** are weights that define the core priorities of the algorithm. To prioritize discourse, β and (1-β) would be significantly larger than γ.
